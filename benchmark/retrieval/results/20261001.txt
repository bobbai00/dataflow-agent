INTERPRETATION
- Flow-off sample+stats costs 4.4% more than full and 10.8% more than sample for Luna; it has 11 full passes, versus 10 and 11. For Terra it costs 13.3% more than full and 19.0% more than sample, with 11 passes versus 12 for both controls. These controls expose no automatic flow evidence; full/sample also expose no data evidence.
- Flow-on sample raises Luna from 11 to 12 full passes at 19.6% more cost. For Terra, the same comparison falls from 12 to 11 at 3.6% less cost. Other policies are mixed. The six-task bootstrap intervals are exploratory and do not establish a robust accuracy improvement.
- End-to-end differences include changes in exploration, graph construction and repairs. Fewer characters per retrieval do not guarantee lower total cost: extra model calls and generated graph edits can offset input savings.
- Cached-input rates are 81.40% for Luna and 80.38% for Terra. All 732 successive input-message prefix checks pass. This provides no evidence of prefix rewriting in these runs; it does not imply every input token is cacheable.
- Five Luna attempts end without an Answer action. Native tool choice is already required; the exact gateway/provider cause is unresolved. Those zero-scored attempts and their costs remain included. This limits attributing small accuracy differences to evidence alone.

TRACE FINDINGS
- Terra full/no-flow biomedical Rep1: correct, four calls, about 76,655 retrieved characters, $0.103329. Wide records drive uncached-input cost. Terra adaptive/flow biomedical Rep2: correct, six calls, $0.225467; two full workbook reads alone contribute 184,433 characters. These show a real cost opportunity, but not a general win for the current statistics policy.
- Terra sample/flow astronomy Rep1 builds sixteen Limit operators to page through text. Its custom parser then overwrites the requested forecast edition and returns 16 instead of 15. Flow labels the custom mapping unknown; it does not detect the hidden semantic mistake.
- Luna adaptive/no-flow legal Rep2 switches the same result from sample (5/8 rows) to full (8/8) without execution. It mistakes the complete eight-row preview for the whole file and answers from military status instead of military branch. The flow-on preview warning is a candidate intervention to test with a controlled replay.
- Several beach failures merge Castle Island into Pleasure Bay inside a parser. Another returns a location suffix that the original scorer penalizes. One has complete correct counts (M Street 77/81) but incorrectly includes it in the final answer. Keep the official scores; the trace audit distinguishes source mapping, exact-name formatting and final comparison errors.

POST-PILOT FIX AND VALIDATION
The trace audit found an inherited renderer problem: when a one-row preview header exceeds the record budget, fallback clipping shows only 120 characters of its raw line. Fixed in 84eb8579d, after the frozen pilot. It now spends the remaining record budget on that line and retains truncation labels.
Replaying the exact saved biomedical header changes its display from 327 to 2,000 characters and exposes Age. No new execution or model call was used. Pilot scores/costs above remain from ab1020138; the fix is not credited with measured accuracy or cost improvements.
Validation: 1,130 unit tests, eight snapshots and TypeScript typecheck pass. Live action/cache/invalidation probes pass. A live 1,000-row retrieval probe returns five identical rows for sample/sample_stats, all 1,000 for full, and the same producing version for all views, with zero additional executions. Both real-model city smoke runs correctly query 44,691 source rows.

NEXT EXPERIMENT
Use paired replays from identical graph/result states with known missingness, join fan-out or preview-coverage mistakes. Vary only the rendered evidence and measure repair quality and total remaining cost. This isolates the information effect before testing broader adaptive policies. Retain held-out tasks and keep constructed cases separate from original KramaBench scores.
