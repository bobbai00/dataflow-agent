KramaBench: where data statistics and flow evidence help
======================================================
Date: 2026-10-01
Review checkout: feat/dataflow-actions, 99f2bfa3a

Conclusion
----------
Both capabilities have real uses. Data statistics describe the values in an
operator's output. Flow evidence describes how that output was obtained:
which rows survived, which keys matched, and how many valid values entered
a reducer. Together they can expose a plausible answer computed over the
wrong population, even when the displayed output is a single number.

The strongest examples are numeric conversion loss, patient eligibility,
categorical filtering, duplicated or incomplete joins, and aggregate
denominators. Existing statistics are often enough to reveal these problems.
They do not ensure that the model repairs them. Temporal alignment, joint
missingness and calculations inside a large Process need more information.

This is a diagnostic review, not a new accuracy experiment. The examples
below are selected after reading outcomes. They identify mechanisms and
testable opportunities; they cannot establish an average accuracy gain.

Scope and evidence
------------------
- All 104 original top-level KramaBench questions, across six domains:
  legal 30, wildfire 21, environment 20, archeology 12, astronomy 12,
  biomedical 9. These are whole questions, not their decomposed subtasks.
- A mechanical census of every available trace in nine recent cohorts:
  1,604 attempts, 1,597 complete traces and seven watchdog partial traces.
  Timeouts remain in the inventory.
- This includes every attempt from the 1,248-job Luna/Terra retrieval/flow
  campaign and the 104-job Haiku campaign, plus 252 pilot/diagnostic attempts.
- 9,406 successful Retrieve observations. The census found data evidence
  in 1,651 observations, flow evidence in 4,014, and upstream flow summaries
  in 1,166. An observation can contain both kinds of evidence.
- 32 selected trace cases examined for specific mechanisms. Independent
  source calculations additionally checked report-count parsing, clinical
  eligibility, beach category spellings and identity, and capital categories.
- All 104 questions have trace coverage; each has at least 13 attempts in
  these cohorts. All have at least one observed flow-evidence exposure;
  103 have an observed data-evidence exposure.

The census checks recorded observations and final profile snapshots. It does
not certify every custom transformation or recompute every intermediate
table. The all-task CSV assigns each question a diagnostic family and lists
useful evidence and gaps; it is not a success/failure judgment.

No model calls, executions, scoring changes or reruns were made for this
review. Historical cohorts retain their original agent/engine revisions.
Their costs and scores are not pooled into a matched model comparison.
Where an example says it passed, its recorded original score was 1.0.
Scores and reference answers come from the existing benchmark artifacts;
this review does not use an LLM judge to relabel success.

Companion files:
- 20261001-evidence-use-cases-tasks.csv: all 104 questions, data sources,
  trace coverage, diagnostic family, useful data/flow facts and limits.
- 20261001-evidence-use-cases.json: cohort provenance, source replays,
  original scores, trace/snapshot paths, producing result versions,
  selected observations and compact final DAGs for the 32 cases.

What we actually compute
-----------------------
Data statistics, over the profiled rows P:
- Null count Z; nonnull count P-Z, separately for each profiled column.
- Distinct nonnull values D while the bounded exact dictionary is retained;
  otherwise distinct is unknown. Unknown is not zero.
- Numeric/datetime minimum and maximum.
- Value frequencies, displaying up to eight values plus an other count.
  An exhaustive list with full row coverage and other=0 can enumerate a
  result column even when the displayed records are incomplete. A top-values
  list is explicitly non-exhaustive. Values across columns are not paired.
- Extra duplicate-row occurrences beyond the first, all-null rows and
  all-null columns when their scopes and collection limits permit.

Generic profiles do not automatically include mean, median, variance,
quantiles, correlations or cross-column validity. Mean, median, stddev and
other supported reductions can be computed explicitly with Aggregate.
Literal strings such as NA or M are not counted as null until normalized.

Collection is bounded independently of the record display: 100,000 rows,
1,000,000 cells, 128 columns, a 0.2-second collection budget and a 64-KiB
payload budget. Exact per-column distinct collection is bounded at 256
values. These are limits, not guarantees of complete coverage. Profiles can
describe a prefix. Retrieving a larger view does not fill uncollected stats.

Flow evidence, from native operator execution and declared contracts:
- Scan: selected/resolved files, omitted files, output rows, source findings.
- Cast: conversion-failure counts per column and the selected error policy.
- Filter: kept/input rows, rejected rows and null predicates; observed
  predicate columns and available input/output column facts.
- Join: matched/unmatched rows and keys on each side, null-key rows,
  maximum eligible multiplicity, and matched output contribution
  sum over keys of L(k)*R(k), plus outer-join contributions.
- Aggregate: grouping keys, input/group counts, min/max group sizes and
  each reducer's nonnull denominator, including min/max per group.
- Distinct/Limit: retained and removed rows. Union: input counts and
  alignment of missing columns.
- Window: row preservation, partitions, ties, and lag/lead boundary-null
  and total-null counts when the Window is directly retrieved.
- Unpivot/Pivot: expansion/group counts and null or absent cells.
- Map: observed input columns, added/dropped columns and available paired
  input/output null counts.
- Process: input/output row counts; its internal row/column mapping is
  unknown. A function's own description is not a verified guarantee.

Current aggregate evidence also includes bounded key-dependency examples,
warnings about unused/dropped flag-like columns, and a grain warning when
count_distinct of a unique row ID is just counting rows. These already
exist; they are not proposed new statistics.

Data and flow evidence have separate display budgets, typically 2,000
characters each, in addition to the 2,000-character record sample. Flow is
automatic on Retrieve when enabled. Selected upstream summaries are bound
to the producing versions, stop at opaque Process calculations, and show
at most three upstream operators within bounded traversal. Window is
currently absent from the upstream summary map, although its direct
retrieval statistics exist.

Choosing a retrieval
--------------------
The existing action is:
  {"type":"retrieve", "operator_id":"eligible", "view":"sample_stats"}
Several such actions can be put in one ordered Dataflows call. Retrieval
reuses execution results. Views must be available in the session.

- sample: headers, example records, formatting/type checks.
- sample_stats: larger parsed intermediate tables, cohort/category checks,
  missingness and ranges. Project relevant columns before execution if a
  wide table hides the needed columns.
- full: a small final list, patient/measurement pairing, contingency table
  or compact diagnostic table whose actual row associations are needed.
  Full remains capped, typically at 100,000 characters. It need not mean
  reading the raw source in full. Small tables can also fit completely in
  sample/sample_stats, in which case extra data facts may be redundant.

Recommendations below describe graph stages, not a new tool grammar. Each
case states observed facts separately from the suggested construction.

Concrete cases: legal report tables
----------------------------------
1. legal-easy-26: states where Identity Theft is the top category
   Data: 2024 state/category/report-count CSV, 524 scanned rows and 520
   data rows. Report counts contain thousands separators.
   Graph: Scan -> remove footer rows -> normalize counts -> Cast ->
   Aggregate maximum by state -> Join categories -> Filter -> final states.
   Retrieve: sample_stats on typed counts; full on the small final list.
   Data use: null/nonnull counts and ranges reveal lost report values.
   Flow use: Cast failures identify the transformation responsible, while
   aggregate denominators show that missing values affected ranking.
   Observed: the original Luna flow run lost 317/520 values and returned
   only four states. Independent replay reduces failures to zero by
   removing commas. The original paired no-flow run normalized counts and
   passed. This is a clear failure mechanism, not evidence of a flow gain.

2. legal-hard-14: top five distinct New England metros by identity theft
   Data: per-state metropolitan CSVs plus six New England state names.
   Graph: Scan -> normalize counts/state keys -> Join eligible states ->
   Aggregate by metro -> Sort -> Limit five.
   Retrieve: sample_stats on new_england_reports and top_five.
   Data use: typed reports had only 11 valid values among 35 joined rows.
   Flow use: the retrieved top five automatically exposed 150/660 Cast
   failures and the sum denominator 11/35. The state Join matched 4/6
   right-side state keys, so geographic coverage was also incomplete.
   Observed: Terra removed commas, raised the valid count to 19/35 and
   changed the top five; the official score passed. The 4/6 state match
   remained. A passing final answer does not certify full state coverage.
   Gap: counts identify the join problem; unmatched-key examples would
   make the remaining state-name normalization error easier to repair.

3. legal-hard-1: average theft reports for metros above one million
   Data: an HTML population table and per-state metro report CSVs;
   populations must be interpolated to 2023 and names robustly matched.
   Graph: narrow parsing Process -> normalize metro key -> Join population
   and reports -> Map interpolated population -> Filter -> Aggregate mean.
   Retrieve: sample_stats on the Join and eligible metros.
   Data use: eligible contained 76 rows but only 55 distinct normalized
   metro keys, with 21 extra duplicate rows. A five-row sample misses this.
   Flow use: Join multiplicity and the final mean denominator expose that
   repeated cross-state metro entries change the weighting.
   Gap: establish metro identity and deduplicate at that grain before the
   mean. Nonnull values alone cannot validate the interpolation formula.

4. legal-hard-29: highest metro share within a state with >=5 metros
   Data: state-specific fraud reports; multi-state metros repeat by file.
   Graph: Scan -> clean/normalize -> Aggregate count and total by state ->
   Filter eligible states -> Join -> Map share -> Sort -> Limit one.
   Retrieve: sample_stats on state totals; full on the winning row.
   Data use: state/source-file distinct counts and report ranges.
   Flow use: grouping keys and sum denominators check that each metro's
   numerator and its denominator belong to the same state representation.
   Observed: 417 cleaned rows, 52 state groups, 35 eligible states and 377
   joined rows. These facts support coverage checks, but do not decide
   whether a source repeats a complete cross-state metro total.

5. legal-hard-30: any metro with more identity-theft than fraud reports?
   Data: two sets of per-state metropolitan tables.
   Graph: clean -> Aggregate at matching metro grain -> Join -> Filter
   identity > fraud -> Count.
   Retrieve: sample_stats on the Join and final count.
   Data use: key distinct counts, numeric validity and duplicate rows.
   Flow use: a zero final count is only convincing if comparison coverage
   is adequate. The trace compared 402 matched rows after constructing
   404 groups on each side; group size reached 104 because headers/footer
   or null keys also entered aggregation. Inspect unmatched groups before
   treating a zero count as proof of absence in all metros.

6. legal-easy-21: Alabama's identity-theft reporter count
   Data: a small state report table with category/detail rows.
   Graph: Scan -> Filter Alabama -> normalize numeric values -> Aggregate.
   Retrieve: full on the seven selected rows or scalar, when available.
   Data use: record labels distinguish a total from its category details.
   Flow use: zero conversion loss and the reducer denominator are useful
   checks. This is a low-benefit control: a small complete result often
   provides enough information without an additional stats block.

7. legal-easy-25: military branch with the largest median fraud loss
   Data: a 23-row sectioned table containing currency-formatted values.
   Graph: Scan -> Map currency normalization -> Filter valid branch rows
   -> Sort -> Limit one. The supplied median is not recomputed from losses.
   Retrieve: full on the 12 valid candidates or the winning row.
   Data use: null/range checks validate parsing; records identify sections.
   Flow use: candidate retention and numeric validity. Again, extra metadata
   has limited value once the small cleaned table is completely visible.

Concrete cases: biomedical cohorts
---------------------------------
8. biomedical-easy-2: average age of serous tumor patients in the study
   Data: clinical workbook, 153 rows and 179 columns, including
   Histologic_type, Proteomics_Tumor_Normal, Case_excluded and Age.
   Graph: Scan -> Project relevant fields -> Filter eligible serous tumor
   samples -> normalize/Cast Age -> Aggregate mean.
   Retrieve: sample_stats on the cohort and the mean.
   Data use: the selected 14 serous rows contained Case_excluded No:12,
   Yes:2. Across the source the flag is No:144, Yes:9.
   Flow use: current diagnostic evidence flagged the unread eligibility
   field; Cast lost 1/14 age values and the mean used 13/14.
   Observed: Luna still answered 68.1. Independent replay gives 68.5 for
   the 12 eligible samples. The evidence exposed the mistake but did not
   change the model's choice. Wide-column omission is a separate problem:
   narrow Project makes the eligibility fields reliably profileable.

9. biomedical-hard-5: median variants per Mbp for serous samples
   Data: clinical and genomic workbook sheets, including a log-scale field.
   Graph: narrow relevant fields -> eligible cohort -> match sample IDs
   where needed -> Map from log scale -> Cast -> Aggregate median.
   Retrieve: sample_stats on eligible typed values; full on the median.
   Data use: eligibility frequencies, valid measurement count and ranges.
   Flow use: cohort retention, sample matching and the median denominator.
   Observed: the trace reduced 14 selected rows using 13 valid values,
   returning 2.4241486 rather than the reference 2.6563. Statistics can
   expose the cohort; they cannot validate units or a 2**log2 conversion.

10. biomedical-hard-1: PLK1/CHEK2-S163 Spearman correlation
    Data: 153 clinical records, 10,999 global-protein rows and 73,212
    phosphoprotein rows; sample measurements are spread across columns.
    Suggested graph: select protein rows -> Unpivot selected measurements
    -> Join by sample ID -> Join eligible clinical IDs -> Filter both
    measurements valid -> narrow Spearman Process.
    Retrieve: sample_stats on aligned pairs and the correlation result.
    Data use: sample-ID distinctness and each measurement's null count.
    Flow use: join multiplicity, eligibility retention and retained pairs.
    Observed: Terra used a Process with inputs [1,1,153] and reported 51
    paired samples. That 51 is function-reported, not independently measured
    by the engine. Current flow evidence sees only input/output counts.
    Gap: joint validity counts, or explicit native pairing/filter stages.

11. biomedical-hard-3: age of the patient with the lowest APP-Z score
    Data: assay scores and clinical ages linked by sample identifier.
    Graph: clean scores -> Join clinical identity -> Sort APP-Z -> Limit.
    Retrieve: sample_stats on matched scores; full on the winning row.
    Data use: APP-Z validity/minimum and identity distinctness.
    Flow use: one-to-one matching, unmatched samples and ties at the minimum.
    Limit: separate minimum-score and age frequencies cannot tell which
    age belongs to which patient. The final record association is needed.

12. biomedical-hard-4: grades of tumors with a specified peptide
    Data: three peptide/variant sheets and clinical metadata.
    Graph: Filter target peptide -> split/normalize associated sample IDs
    if compound -> select or Join clinical IDs -> Project/Distinct grades.
    Retrieve: sample_stats on matches; full on the compact ID/grade rows.
    Data use: sample-ID nulls and grade frequencies.
    Flow use: peptide-filter retention, expansion counts, match coverage
    and Distinct removal. The selected trace found the peptide in one
    alternate-splice row and selected three clinical records. ID expansion
    hidden in a Process would need an exposed result to audit its mapping.

13. biomedical-hard-8: drug-targeted hyperactivated sites in CNV-high tumors
    Data: clinical subtype/eligibility, hyperactivation and FDA drug targets.
    Graph: Filter eligible CNV-high endometrioid samples -> Join
    hyperactivation -> Join drug targets -> Project/Distinct target sites.
    Retrieve: sample_stats after each important join; full on final sites.
    Data use: CNV/histology/eligibility categories, sample/protein/gene IDs.
    Flow use: each join's matching/multiplicity and cohort retention.
    Observed: the trace selected nine clinical rows, expanded to 46
    target-joined rows and reduced to two gene labels. Statistics can reveal
    the expansion but cannot equate gene labels with the requested sites.

Concrete cases: environmental measurements
------------------------------------------
14. environment-easy-1: 2013 percentage exceeding bacterial standards
    Data: 15,388 beach test records with Violation labels.
    Graph: Scan -> normalize labels -> Aggregate total and exceedance
    counts -> Map percentage.
    Retrieve: sample_stats on normalized labels; full on the scalar.
    Data use: exhaustive values yes:738, no:14,650 establish the numerator.
    Flow use: row denominator 15,388 shows whether all tests were used.
    Independent result: 738/15,388*100 = 4.795944892..., rounding to 4.796.
    Limit: statistics do not perform the final rounding on the agent's
    behalf. Computing the ratio in the graph avoids manual arithmetic.

15. environment-easy-2: freshwater years above the 2002-2023 average
    Data: 22 annual files, 319,911 tests; freshwater cohort 154,000 rows.
    Graph: normalize -> Filter freshwater -> Aggregate annual counts ->
    compute specified baseline -> Filter higher-rate years.
    Retrieve: sample_stats on annual rates and the final year list.
    Data use: the final eight-row result displayed five records but its
    full-coverage Year frequencies enumerated all eight with other=0:
    2003, 2011, 2015, 2018, 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023.
    Observed: Haiku still made four more calls before Answer. This is a
    concrete opportunity to avoid redundant retrieval, not an observed saving.
    Flow use: annual denominators and grouping keys; pooled test rates and
    an unweighted mean of annual rates are different calculations. Exhaustive
    year values certify the returned list, not the baseline formula.

16. environment-easy-3: beaches worsening between 2012 and 2013
    Data: annual test records with BeachName and geographic identity fields.
    Graph: normalize location identity -> Aggregate rate by location/year ->
    Join comparable years -> Filter increase -> Count.
    Retrieve: sample_stats on location-level rates and the comparison Join.
    Data use: independent replay finds 1,155 lowercased names versus 1,171
    location keys in 2012; 14 names refer to multiple locations. Sandy Beach
    and Crystal Lake each occur in three locations.
    Flow use: grouping keys, group sizes and join coverage reveal what a
    row represents. Luna grouped by name alone, compared 1,124 rows and
    returned 267 instead of reference 268. A formally one-to-one join can
    still operate on the wrong entity definition.
    Gap: requested composite-key diagnostics; existing aggregate
    key-dependency examples help but do not choose the intended identity.

17. environment-hard-12: beach failures with zero preceding three-day rain
    Data: eight beach sheets with multiple sampling-point columns and rain.
    Graph: Scan -> Unpivot sampling points -> normalize/Cast measurements
    and rain -> Filter dry failures -> Aggregate by beach.
    Retrieve: sample_stats after reshaping and typing; full on beach counts.
    Data use: rain numeric validity, missing/censored markers and bacterial
    ranges. A string "0" is not automatically numeric zero in a predicate.
    Flow use: Cast failures, Unpivot expansion and Filter retention locate
    lost/misclassified samples. The trace's final union had 23,292 rows.
    Limit: the wording/reference differ at the threshold equality boundary;
    stats cannot decide whether the intended failure condition includes 104.

18. environment-hard-13: 2024 days when Constitution stations disagree
    Data: per-date values for several sampling points.
    Graph: Filter year -> normalize point values -> classify each valid
    point -> Aggregate by date -> Filter mixed valid outcomes -> Count.
    Retrieve: sample_stats on point results and daily groups.
    Data use: Date/Location distinctness and bacterial null counts.
    Flow use: group grain and nonnull versus all-row denominators distinguish
    a missing station from a passing station. If Unpivot is used, its row
    count checks the expected point expansion. The trace produced 11 mixed
    days from 84 dated rows. Threshold semantics still need explicit logic.

19. environment-hard-15: most polluted freshwater beach measured every year
    Data: 2020-2023 test records; rank by average annual exceedance rate.
    Graph: Filter freshwater -> Aggregate by beach/year -> Map annual rate
    -> Aggregate average plus count_distinct(year) by beach -> Filter four
    years -> Sort -> Limit one.
    Retrieve: sample_stats on annual rates and the complete-year cohort.
    Data use: year values and missing labels.
    Flow use: 29,589 freshwater rows -> 2,189 annual groups -> 607 beaches
    -> 448 complete-year beaches; group sizes ranged from one to four years.
    Global coverage of four years does not prove each beach has four years.
    An explicit per-beach distinct-year count makes the eligibility test clear.

20. environment-hard-18: freshwater exceedance and rainfall trends
    Data: four annual beach tables and monthly precipitation sheets;
    missing rainfall is imputed with the month's median in nonmissing years.
    Graph: normalize flags/missing markers -> annual test rates;
    Unpivot rainfall -> month-specific imputation -> annual rainfall ->
    Join years -> compare changes.
    Retrieve: sample_stats on normalized flags, rain and joined annual values.
    Data use: 2020 freshwater labels No:6,163/Yes:363; 2021 NO:7,101/YES:505.
    These exact frequencies expose a case-sensitive filter that misses 2021.
    Rain marker M is a string until normalized.
    Flow use: conversion failures, all-year join coverage and sum/mean
    denominators. Statistics cannot certify the imputation rule inside code.

22. archeology-easy-8: number of unique bibliography sources
    Data: 1,388 Roman-city rows; Bibliography has 1,385 nonnull cells,

23. archeology-hard-7: populous modern cities near ancient Roman cities

Concrete cases: wildfire data
----------------------------
24. wildfire-easy-1: largest consecutive three-month acreage total
    Data: 303 NOAA monthly rows from 2000 onward.
    Graph: Sort date -> Window lag(offset=1) and lag(offset=2) -> Map sum ->
    Filter complete windows -> Sort -> Limit one.
    Retrieve: sample_stats directly on Window outputs; full on winning
    month, its two predecessor dates/values and the computed sum.
    Data use: date coverage, null counts and acreage ranges.
    Flow use: lag boundary-null counts should distinguish one from two
    missing predecessors. The original graph accidentally used lag 1 twice;
    it retained 302/303 windows and displayed prev1=prev2=3,461,087, producing
    9,397,894. The reference total is 7,805,421.
    Gap: expose existing Window counters upstream; additionally check that
    predecessors are consecutive calendar months, not merely adjacent rows.

25. wildfire-hard-16: regional fire-cause chi-square test in January-March
    Data: 6,658 fires with date, region and cause codes.
    Graph: Filter known causes/Q1 -> Aggregate counts by region/cause ->
    Pivot/fill absent cells with zero -> narrow chi-square Process.
    Retrieve: sample_stats on eligible fires; full on the small contingency
    table. Six regions and three cause codes yield a compact 6x3 table.
    Data use: cause/region frequencies and valid category counts.
    Flow use: filter kept 170/6,658; Aggregate emitted ten nonempty cells,
    with group sizes 1-118. Frequencies of the ten aggregated rows count
    cells, not fire events. They do not reconstruct the contingency matrix.
    Gap: expected-cell minimum and count below five would help assess the
    test; they would not by themselves establish statistical validity.

26. wildfire-hard-17: average elevation of monitoring stations actually used
    Data: fire records with station IDs and a 2,965-row RAWS station table.
    Graph: Project fire station IDs -> normalize/Distinct -> Join station
    metadata -> Aggregate mean elevation.
    Retrieve: sample_stats on used-station matches and the mean.
    Data use: station-ID distinctness and elevation validity/range.
    Flow use: join coverage and the denominator distinguish one vote per
    station from one vote per fire, or averaging the entire station table.
    Observed: Terra averaged all 2,965 stations using 2,949 nonnull elevations,
    without linking fire usage. Caveat: the reference script reads a different
    fire filename from the supplied task; this is a diagnostic example,
    not a clean reference-based accuracy exhibit.

27. wildfire-hard-20: fraction of fires responsible for 90% of damage
    Data: 2008 fire rows and a residential-property damage proxy.
    Graph: Filter year -> Sort damage descending -> narrow cumulative-sum
    Process -> expose threshold crossing and counts -> ratio.
    Retrieve: sample_stats on typed damage; full on the threshold witness.
    Data use: damage nulls, zero frequencies and numeric range.
    Flow use: year retention (473 rows in the trace), valid-damage count and
    an explicit all-eligible-fire denominator. Native Window does not support
    cumulative sum; lag/lead/rank are its current functions.
    Gap: report cumulative damage/total and number of rows required at the
    crossing. Reference caveat: its script uses an original row index as a
    count and presents a fraction as a percentage; passing is not proof that
    the intended statistic is correct.

28. wildfire-easy-15: July fire size versus median wind correlation
    Data: NOAA dates, fire hectares and wind speed.
    Graph: Map calendar month from start_date -> Filter month==7 ->
    Project measurements -> Filter jointly valid -> correlation Process.
    Retrieve: sample_stats on the July cohort and paired measurements.
    Data use: month distribution should be July only; both measurements'
    null/range checks help but do not establish paired validity.
    Flow use: cohort retention and explicit paired-row denominator.
    Observed: Haiku used a fixed day-of-year interval and selected 2,003
    fires; calendar July has 2,000. Fifteen June 30 records leaked in and
    twelve July 31 records were lost in leap years. Both answers were No,
    so the official score did not reveal the defect. The original reference
    also uses an approximate day interval; no score was altered here.
    Gap: calendar-bin and joint-validity diagnostics.

29. wildfire-hard-19: control-day rain within a fire's assumed area
    Data: 6,658 fires plus 8,627,181 weather-event rows, about 1.09 GB.
    Graph: Filter year/date/rain -> narrow spatial/temporal match Process ->
    expose fire matches -> Distinct fire ID -> Aggregate fraction.
    Retrieve: sample_stats on narrowed rain events and per-fire matches;
    full on a few distance/date witnesses.
    Data use: precipitation/date/coordinate validity. A prefix of the large
    weather table cannot establish global absence or full-year distribution.
    Flow use: filters reduced weather to 291,529 events in 2016 and fires
    to 479; explicit per-fire outputs can expose duplicate counting.
    Gap: match-distance and time-offset witnesses, unmatched fire counts,
    and honest profile coverage. Statistics do not repair resource timeouts
    or verify the spatial calculation hidden inside Process.

Concrete cases: satellite alignment
-----------------------------------
30. astronomy-easy-3: 2015 midnight density at 450-500 km initial altitude
    Data: 715 initial-state rows and 53,259 satellite density rows;
    n/a and 9.99E32 are invalid measurements.
    Graph: normalize density -> Filter eligible initial states -> normalize
    time/file keys -> Join aligned measurements -> Aggregate mean.
    Retrieve: sample_stats on cleaned density and the Join/mean.
    Data use: density range reveals fill values; time/file distinctness
    and valid counts help validate cleaning.
    Flow use: initial selection kept 120; cleaned density kept 52,817.
    The first Join matched zero rows. Relaxing to time alone produced 238
    rows, with right-side multiplicity two; deduplicating yielded 119.
    Observed: Terra saw these facts but still failed. Making a join nonempty
    does not establish correct satellite/run identity.
    Gap: bounded unmatched time/file keys and match-offset witnesses.

31. astronomy-hard-9: best 0-48-hour drag/AP correlation lag
    Data: TLE orbital states and hourly OMNI AP measurements in May 2024.
    Graph: parse TLE -> derive semi-major axis -> Sort/Window difference ->
    round epochs -> Aggregate hourly -> narrow lag evaluation Process.
    Retrieve: sample_stats on hourly changes/AP; full on the 49-row lag
    comparison when its coefficients and sample counts are needed.
    Data use: measurement ranges, constants, timestamp validity.
    Flow use: hourly group sizes, boundary nulls and alignment matches where
    the joins are explicit. The trace had 133 selected TLE changes reduced
    to 125 hours, versus 720 OMNI hours, then an opaque 49-row Process.
    Gap: valid-pair count and time overlap for each lag. Different lags can
    otherwise be compared using different effective populations.

32. astronomy-hard-10: strongest satellite hourly-altitude correlation
    Data: SP3 position records, OMNI variables and density for Oct 1-10.
    Graph: narrow SP3 parsing -> Map altitude/time -> Aggregate hourly ->

Are the current statistics enough?
----------------------------------
Enough to expose many input/population mistakes:
- Nulls/nonnulls plus Cast failures explain the comma conversion loss.
- Eligibility frequencies plus unread-field warnings explain the serous
  cohort error. They were delivered in a repaired diagnostic and ignored.
- Capital categories plus Distinct counts explain the wrong capital cohort.
- Exact Join matching/multiplicity and group denominators expose empty,
  incomplete or duplicated comparisons.
- Exhaustive full-coverage value frequencies can completely identify a
  final answer column, as in the eight-year freshwater result.

Not enough to prove the analysis:
- Marginal profiles do not establish paired missingness or row associations.
- A full profile can accurately describe the wrong population.
- Generic time ranges do not prove consecutive months, calendar membership,
  overlap at a specified lag or correct interpolation.
- A Process can output a correct-looking scalar while hiding every internal
  eligibility filter and join. More prose cannot reconstruct those stages.
- A small display budget can omit collected facts; collection limits can
  prevent relevant facts from existing at all. These are different problems.

Coverage findings:
- 160 successful observations explicitly reported partial-prefix statistics;
  21 explicitly reported unavailable statistics. Early pilot renderers used
  different headers, so these are recognized occurrences, not a universal
  prevalence estimate across all revisions.
- Among 15,508 final profiles, 1,431 had partial row coverage and 318 did not
  profile every known column. Another 73 lacked a known columnCount field.
  Final snapshots do not include every earlier profile/version.
- Earlier full-campaign payload/frequency omissions were already investigated
  and partly repaired. Do not attribute all historical omissions to current
  code or interpret a later Haiku payload as the same revision as Luna/Terra.

Recommended improvements for review
-----------------------------------
First expose and teach the existing measurements better:
1. Add short worked examples with a real observation and the corresponding
   repair: conversion loss -> normalize; unused exclusion flag -> inspect
   eligibility; no join matches -> inspect keys. Current prompt text explains
   these capabilities but does not work through all three retrieval views or
   a complete evidence-to-repair sequence. Also show an exhaustive final
   value list as a sufficient stopping condition when row pairing is irrelevant.
2. Carry existing Window boundary counters and resolved lag offsets in
   selected upstream summaries. Currently only direct Window retrieval shows
   these measurements. Preserve bounded, producing-version provenance.
3. Encourage narrow native cohort, join and aggregation stages around a
   specialized Process. This gives the engine meaningful points to measure;
   it does not require implementing every scientific calculation natively.

Then add small, targeted information:
4. Cast witnesses: up to three distinct raw values that failed conversion,
   with counts, next to the existing failure total. Examples would distinguish
   comma formatting, currency, NA markers and genuinely invalid values.
5. Join witnesses: up to three unmatched keys per side and a few duplicate
   keys with multiplicities. Counts already exist; the examples are new.
   Let callers request composite-key checks at the task's entity grain.
6. Joint validity for requested measurement pairs: both valid, X only,
   Y only, neither, and eligible rows. This is more informative for correlation
   than two independent null counts. It can already be computed with explicit
   Filter/Aggregate stages; optional profiling would reduce that work.
7. Temporal diagnostics: missing expected months/hours, gap counts,
   calendar-month distribution and join time-offset examples. Min/max and
   lag boundary counts already exist; these continuity/alignment facts do not.
8. Requested-column collection for wide tables, with explicit coverage.
   A 179-column sheet needs reliable eligibility/measurement profiles beyond
   the first 128 columns. Increasing a display budget cannot recover a column
   that was never collected. Projection is the current workaround.

Lower priority, useful for particular tasks:
- Bounded quantiles/zero mass for skewed damage or loss distributions.
- Expected chi-square cell diagnostics, feature-specific correlation pair
  counts, and explicit time/units metadata. Distinguish engine-derived facts
  from a custom function's self-reported diagnostics.
- Version-to-version changes for selected fields: cast loss 317 -> 0, cohort
  14 -> 12, or join coverage before/after normalization. Existing version
  tracking permits the comparison; a concise rendered difference is new.

How to test the research claim
-----------------------------
Use a fixed task selection and revision for new matched comparisons. Keep
the official KramaBench evaluator unchanged and additionally audit whether
the intended cohort, identity, temporal alignment and formula were used.

Data-statistics pilots: legal-easy-26 (conversion), biomedical-easy-2
(eligibility), archeology-easy-11 (categories), environment-hard-18 (case
variation/missing markers), environment-easy-2 (complete values without
complete displayed records). Compare full, sample and sample_stats with
the same evidence-aware guidance and independent record/stats budgets.
Include legal-easy-21/easy-25 as small-table controls.

Flow pilots: legal-hard-1 (duplicate weighting), legal-hard-14 (conversion
and state coverage), environment-hard-15 (year eligibility), astronomy-easy-3
(join alignment), wildfire-easy-1 (lag offsets). Hold retrieval view fixed
and toggle automatic flow evidence. Evaluate the evidence-to-repair event,
not only final score. These cases were selected after outcomes, so label
them diagnostic pilots and use a separate selection for confirmatory claims.

Record: official score, total/cache-adjusted provider cost, input/output
tokens, calls/executions/retrievals, bytes per evidence kind, coverage,
whether a relevant warning was shown, and whether the next edit addressed
it. A one-repetition comparison can describe a run; it cannot establish
stable average improvements or distinguish them from stochastic variation.
